# Budel Craft measured results (0.2.0)

All 0.2.0 measurements ran on a 2-vCPU sandbox (Intel Xeon @ 2.10 GHz, Linux 6.18) with Rust 1.97.0, and Python 3.13 with NumPy 2.5.3 and Pillow 12.3.0 for the reference judge. In the final campaign each run used one worker pinned to one CPU, and the two campaign processes used separate CPUs (`--cpu-offset 0` and `1`). The 0.1.0 records under `evidence/final` came from a different host (Intel Xeon Platinum 8573C, Rust 1.88.0). Compare versions with the same-host `multistart` arm below, not across hosts.

The 14 s medium arms, the 6 s medium arms other than `m6-gated`, and the layered-hard arms other than `lh-gated` ran before `rescue_after` existed. They ran pockets and separation every round (`rescue=0`), used `late_length` 128 unless `strict` is shown, and used `separate_moves` 240. The `gated` arms used the final defaults with `--strict-accept`.

- **Slide compaction is the largest single component.** Removing it costs 1.3 points on medium at 14 s (49.58% against 50.87% for the late-acceptance arm it was removed from).
- **Late acceptance hurts.** On medium at 14 s the same configuration averages 51.47% without it and 50.87% with it. In policy tuning every candidate with `late_length` > 0 scored 94.6–96.6% of available stock, against 97.3–98.3% without it. The default is `late_length = 0`; the operator stays available through the policy.
- **Pockets and separation are neutral at interactive budgets.** On layered-hard at 65 s all four combinations average 50.33–50.43%, with seed-to-seed spreads of about 0.5 points. Gating them behind two stalled rounds (`rescue_after = 2`, `separate_moves = 600`) gave the best 6 s medium mean (51.02% against 50.90% for slide only) and tied on layered-hard (50.41% against 50.43%). They stay on, gated, because they target stagnation; the measurements do not establish a gain.
- **The certification mailbox matters.** The prototype's persistent-plus-slide search reached 51.18 / 51.19% on medium (seeds 1/2). The same search with the non-blocking mailbox (`slideonly`) reached 51.52 / 51.585 / 51.585%. In 0.1.0 a worker blocked whenever two candidates were waiting for validation.

## Policy tuning

`budel tune` on medium, 1,200,000 proposals per trial, 5 training seeds (11–15) per candidate, 5 held-out seeds, 16 evaluations per method. Raw reports are in `evidence/v0.2.0/tuning/`. Values are mean percentages of available stock area.

| Method | Selected on training | Default on training | Selected on held-out | Default on held-out |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| BiteOpt (fcmaes-core 0.1.3) | 98.268% | 97.719% | 97.817% | 98.163% |
| Random | default | 97.719% | 98.163% | 98.163% |

BiteOpt's selection did not hold up on the held-out seeds, and random search found nothing better than the default, so the default policy is unchanged. The per-seed spread of one policy (about 0.5–1.3 points) exceeds the differences between the better candidates at this evaluation budget.

## Earlier prototype

The first review patched 0.1.0 with environment switches (`evidence/v0.2.0/prototype-0.1.0-env.json`, same sandbox). Persistent search raised medium at 14 s from 47.53 / 47.19% to 50.96 / 51.10%, and adding slide compaction raised it to 51.18 / 51.19%. On layered-hard at 65 s, seed 1 rose from 46.26% to 49.26% (persistent) and 49.94% (with slide). The 0.2.0 implementation replaces the switches with configuration fields and adds the components above.

## Limits

Three seeds per configuration support the large effects: persistent search, sliding, the mailbox and the harm from late acceptance. They do not separate differences below about 0.3 points. The feasible optimum for layered-hard is unknown, and its remaining gap is measured against the stock upper bound. Timings depend on this host; the reference judge runs separately and is not solver time.

0.1.0 records remain in `evidence/final`, `evidence/pilots`, `evidence/experiments` and `evidence/v0.1.0-answers`. The 0.1.0 log is in [IMPLEMENTATION_LOG_0.1.0.md](IMPLEMENTATION_LOG_0.1.0.md).
